The situation

A customer suddenly makes several larger deposits after a history of more modest activity. An interaction is arranged. During the conversation, frustration and references to recovering losses raise concerns about control and continued play.

01

Notice the change

A customer with a modest deposit history makes several larger deposits in a short period.

What I would check

I compare the change with their previous pattern, session length, losses, failed attempts, existing limits and previous interactions.

Action and record

Record the change and arrange a customer-protection review. Higher spending alone does not establish harm.

02

Create room for a conversation

The activity suggests that continued play may increase the concern.

What I would check

I check what protective action is supported by policy and what authority is available. A restriction is a protective measure, not a punishment.

Action and record

Apply or maintain a temporary restriction where appropriate, and document why interaction is needed.

03

Make contact clearly

The customer needs a clear route to discuss the concern.

What I would check

I keep the contact request calm, direct and neutral. I explain that the team needs a conversation about a change in account activity.

Action and record

Record the request and the interaction requirement, without accusatory language or a premature conclusion.

04

Listen through the pushback

The customer calls, frustrated, and asks for immediate access.

What I would check

I acknowledge the frustration, ask open questions and listen to the explanation for the deposits. I pay attention to urgency, financial pressure and how the customer describes control.

Action and record

Keep the conversation respectful and the protective measure under review while the concern is assessed.

05

Reflect the concern

The customer describes chasing losses and difficulty taking a break.

What I would check

I distinguish what the customer actually says from what I infer. I explain the concern plainly and discuss protective options without turning the conversation into an argument.

Action and record

Consider stronger support such as a time-out or self-exclusion where the conversation and policy support it.

06

Agree and document a safer step

The customer recognises that taking a break may help.

What I would check

I check understanding of the proposed action, its effect, the need for further escalation and the support information to provide.

Action and record

In this illustration, a protective break is agreed and recorded. Where concerns remain unresolved, restrictions or further review may still be necessary.

PROPORTIONATE ACTION

The evidence shapes the outcome.

Monitor

A reasonable explanation and no wider concern may support restoration with monitoring.

Use protective tools

Limits, reminders or time-outs may fit the level of concern.

Maintain restriction

Unresolved concerns require continued protection and review.

Take a break

Cooling-off or self-exclusion may be appropriate and clearly explained.

Escalate

Significant concerns need further review or safeguarding support through the appropriate route.

WHAT THIS DEMONSTRATES

A calm conversation is part of the work.

Notice the change, listen without judgement, explain the concern and document the action. Protecting the customer matters more than winning the argument.
